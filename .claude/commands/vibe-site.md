# Positioning with AI — Prompting Techniques

Prompting techniques for arriving at differentiated positioning using Claude Code as a strategic thinking partner.

## The Sequence

### 1. Dump raw context, not a brief
Feed the AI everything: LinkedIn profile, business plan PDF, existing site, raw notes. Don't pre-digest. The AI finds patterns you've stopped seeing.

**Prompt pattern:** "Read this. Tell me what you learn."

### 2. Apply a named framework
Don't ask "help me with positioning." Ask the AI to apply a specific framework. Named frameworks constrain the output usefully.

**Prompt pattern:** "Seth Godin has the positioning statement that goes 'This product is for those who believe XX.' Apply this to my business."

### 3. Fill context with competitors
Fetch 4-6 competitor websites into the context window. The AI will spot the clustering that's invisible when you look at competitors one at a time.

**Prompt pattern:** "Take your base training. How about [competitors]? What is the contrast there?"

Use WebFetch to pull live competitor sites. The anti-branding table ("The usual" vs "Us") emerges from this.

### 4. Push back on the first answer
The first positioning attempt will be too vague. If nobody would disagree with it, it's not positioning. Say "nah" and describe what you're feeling, even loosely.

**Prompt pattern:** "nah. I'm not feeling this. Something about [vague direction]."

This is the most important prompt in the sequence. Your instinct carries signal the AI can't generate alone.

### 5. Test with the disagreement test
A positioning belief must split the room. If a McKinsey partner or a Gofore seller would nod along, it's not sharp enough.

**Prompt pattern:** "Would [competitor] disagree with this? If not, sharpen it."

### 6. Map the quadrant
Ask for a visual map of where competitors cluster and where the empty space is. ASCII works. The empty quadrant is the positioning.

**Prompt pattern:** "Draw the differentiation as ASCII."

### 7. Explore the blue ocean forward
Once positioning is locked, ask the AI to reason about structural trends. What gives this position a tailwind? What makes it impossible for competitors to follow?

**Prompt pattern:** "What's the blue ocean for me with [trend]? Can you guess?"

"Can you guess?" is powerful — it gives the AI permission to speculate and synthesise, not just summarise.

### 8. Make it real
Only now build the site. The positioning does the structural work. The site is just the expression.

**Prompt pattern:** "Make this happen."

## Key Principles

- **Context window is your strategy room.** Fill it with competitors, your own materials, frameworks. The AI reasons across all of it simultaneously.
- **Your pushback is the most valuable input.** "Nah" followed by a vague feeling produces better positioning than a detailed brief.
- **Named frameworks beat open-ended questions.** "Apply Godin" beats "help me position."
- **The disagreement test is non-negotiable.** If everyone agrees, you're in the crowded quadrant.
- **Don't polish before you've found the edge.** Content before design. Belief before copy.
