Positioning with AI: 8 prompting techniques that actually work

How to use Claude Code as a strategic thinking partner to arrive at differentiated positioning — not generic consulting copy.

Antti Tevanlinna · LinkedIn · tevanlin@gmail.com
01

Dump raw context, not a brief

Feed the AI everything: LinkedIn profile, business plan PDF, existing site. Don't pre-digest. Don't summarise. The AI finds patterns you've stopped seeing because you're too close.

Prompt
"Read this. Tell me what you learn."

A brief tells the AI what you already think. Raw data lets it see what you've missed.

02

Apply a named framework

Don't ask "help me with positioning." Ask the AI to apply a specific, named framework. Constraints produce better output than open-ended requests.

Prompt
"Seth Godin has the positioning statement that goes 'This product is for those who believe XX.' Know that? Apply it."

Named frameworks give the AI a structure to fill, not a void to wander in. The output is immediately testable against the framework's own rules.

03

Fill the context window with competitors

Fetch 4-6 competitor websites into the conversation. The AI sees the clustering that's invisible when you look at them one at a time. Every competitor saying "sustainable transformation" is a signal.

Prompt
"Let's do anti-branding. Fill up the context window with competitors. Think of [Gofore, CGI, Sofigate...]. Compare and contrast my style."

The context window becomes your strategy room. The AI reasons across all competitors simultaneously — something a human can only do with post-it notes and a wall.

04

Push back with feeling, not logic

The first positioning attempt will be too vague. Don't explain why it's wrong — just say what you're feeling, even loosely. Your instinct carries signal the AI can't generate alone.

Prompt
"Nah. I'm not feeling this. Something about actioning now."

This is the most important prompt in the sequence. "Nah" plus a vague direction produces better positioning than a detailed brief ever could. You're the compass. The AI is the engine.

05

Apply the disagreement test

A positioning belief must split the room. If a McKinsey partner would nod along, it's not sharp enough. If nobody would argue with it, it's a bumper sticker, not a position.

Prompt
"Would [competitor] disagree with this belief? If not, sharpen it."

"Believe in progress" fails — who doesn't? "Believe the answer is in the doing, not the planning" passes — Deloitte's entire business model disagrees.

06

Map the quadrant

Ask for a visual map of where competitors cluster and where the empty space is. The empty quadrant is the positioning. If you're alone there, check whether the economics prevent competitors from following.

Prompt
"Draw the differentiation as ASCII."

A quadrant makes the abstract spatial. You can literally see the crowded zone and the open water. It also tests whether your positioning is truly differentiated or just differently worded.

07

Explore the blue ocean

Once positioning is locked, ask the AI to reason about structural trends. What gives this position a tailwind? What makes it impossible for competitors to follow?

Prompt
"What's the blue ocean for me with AI agents and the future? Can you guess?"

"Can you guess?" gives the AI permission to speculate and synthesise, not just summarise. It often produces the most differentiated insight in the session.

08

Make it real

Only now build the site. The positioning does the structural work. The site is just the expression. Commit at checkpoints so you have the safety to be bold.

Prompt
"Make this happen."

Three words. The positioning is done. The thinking is done. Now it's about doing — which, if the positioning is right, is exactly the point.

Principles

Context window = strategy room

Fill it with competitors, your materials, frameworks. The AI reasons across all of it simultaneously.

Your pushback is the best input

"Nah" followed by a vague feeling produces better positioning than a detailed brief.

Named frameworks beat open questions

"Apply Godin" beats "help me position." Constraints produce creativity.

The disagreement test is mandatory

If everyone agrees with your belief statement, you're in the crowded quadrant.

Don't polish before you find the edge

Content before design. Belief before copy. The site is the expression, not the thinking.

Commit early, commit often

Checkpoints create safety to be bold. You can always go back, so you're never afraid to go forward.
